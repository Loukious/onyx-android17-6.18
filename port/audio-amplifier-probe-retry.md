Onyx can stall at the boot animation when AW882xx and SIA amplifier alternatives probe concurrently. Both families share their left and right reset GPIOs. If SIA is still detecting its chip, AW may see a temporary busy reset GPIO. The port previously returned that error permanently, leaving one AW amplifier unbound; the machine sound card then waited for its missing amplifier and Android audio startup could not finish.

Patch 0243 keeps every GPIO request exclusive and converts a busy AW reset, interrupt or speaker-switch request to deferred probe. Other GPIO errors remain unchanged, and the diagnostic includes the affected GPIO. The patch also lets the driver core remove SIA's managed consumer link after failed/deferred probes, removing the invalid manual deletion that warned on Linux 6.18.

The observed failure was confirmed on the development phone: after its left reset GPIO became free, explicitly rebinding the unbound AW amplifier registered `tuna-mtp-snd-card` and Android completed boot without rebooting. PAL's missing resource-XML messages followed the absent sound card and its fallback filename selection; no XML change was required for recovery. This identifies the boot stall, while the original unexpected reboot requires separate crash evidence.

The source-based GPIO harness passed 1,000 presence/error combinations and the observed busy-then-released retry sequence. Independent driver-core review confirms that partial managed GPIO requests and SIA consumer links are cleaned before retry. The complete modified module snapshot reproduces from pinned imports and reviewed patches; its host receipt is [audio-amplifier-probe-retry-host-validation.json](audio-amplifier-probe-retry-host-validation.json).

The official two-target Kleaf build passed. All 185 imports match the retained installed kernel and providers, and all 17 SIA exports keep their CRC, kind and namespace. The focused update was installed in the current EvolutionX 17.0 ROM, replacing matching module copies in vendor_boot, vendor_dlkm and recovery while retaining the 6.18.38 GKI and KernelSU Next. All nine partition readbacks and both live module build IDs matched the final manifest.

Two fresh Android boots registered both AW amplifiers and `tuna-mtp-snd-card` automatically. Boot completion was observed by 22.24 and 20.62 seconds. Audio and system services remained healthy at 156.81 seconds on the first boot and 92.35 seconds on the second; captured logs show no recurrence of the audio watchdog failure or SIA managed-link warning. The installed recovery also reached fastbootd, and the phone was returned to Android. The device receipt is [audio-amplifier-probe-retry-device-validation.json](audio-amplifier-probe-retry-device-validation.json).

That checkpoint's matched vendor_boot also changed `androidboot.load_modules_parallel=true` to `false`. Serial first-stage loading restored recovery startup; those boots validated the combined configuration. The later [kernel readiness repair](parallel-module-loading.md) supersedes that workaround. Both the BoardConfig workaround and the separate ROM loader patch have been removed. Original-loader validation and the retained amplifier fixes are recorded in that report. Hardware acceptance covers this phone with two AW amplifiers; SIA-only variants and the original spontaneous crash remain unverified.
